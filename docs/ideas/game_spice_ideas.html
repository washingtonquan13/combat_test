Game Spice Ideas

These are the things that move the prototype past white-boxing. They are listed in the suggested build order: cheapest and most audible first, riskiest last.

"We need to move on from white boxing … in short, we need the spice that comes with games."

  1. Sound effects

    idea

    Footsteps by surface, weapon swings and impacts, spell casts, UI clicks, doors, and props being dragged.

    This gives the most game feel for the least work. It needs a small SFX bus, and the sources must be CC0 or generated.

  2. BG3-like ambient soundtrack

    started

    A generated exploration score built for the sfpp engine. It uses intensity layers, a loop with exits, reverb spaces per bus, and transition rules between areas.

    The first draft uses libraries already on disk: VPO, VSCO2 and VCSL.

  3. Environmental interactivity

    idea

    Surfaces that react, props that can be broken or pushed, levers, hidden stashes, and objects that respond to fire, water and lightning.

    This builds on movable props, surfaces, and Jump/Shove, which already exist.

  4. Customizable character models

    idea

    Real character models with swappable heads, hair, body and colors in place of placeholder figures.

    Model and animation are already decoupled, so new bodies can reuse the existing animations through retargeting.

  5. Cinematics with markerless mocap

    idea

    Dialogue and story shots driven by motion captured from video with no suits or markers, played through the existing cinematic director.

    No Mixamo. This depends on having real character models first.

  6. Procedural voices as placeholders

    idea

    Generated barks and dialogue lines that hold the place of real voice acting.

    These are placeholders. The voice prefetch plan, saved for later, covers streaming them.

  7. The spice that comes with games

    open

    The catch-all: small reactions, juice, ambient life, and the details that make a world feel inhabited.

    Add to this item as ideas come up.

Ground rule

Aim each piece at one short, polished stretch of play, such as the goblin quarry, rather than spreading it thin across every area.
