The architecture is the right one. Typed pools say something GURPS's maneuver list only implies: legs and hands are different budgets, and standing still is what buys extra actions. It's also the system GURPS's own designer drafted first, abandoned only because counting points by hand was tedious, which a computer removes. It loses points because, as written, the baseline turn is a free Move and Attack, extra attacks cost nothing but AP, and speed becomes an attack stat. All three are closed by the fixes below without leaving the typed-pool design.

The dock with AP

Does movement still get a bar?Yes, the same gold bar.

Movement is spent per metre, so it's fractional; a bar shows that honestly where blocks can't. It's the bar you already approved, now also the Movement AP pool. When an action borrows from it, the borrowed chunk is hatched orange.

Is AP a bar or pips?Pips for actions, a bar for movement.

General, Attack and Spell AP are small whole numbers, and spending one is a decision, so they're diamond pips beside the bar, one colour per pool. Movement AP stays continuous. Two shapes, so the eye never confuses legs with hands.

How do you see what an action costs?A ghost preview.

Hover or tap an ability: the pip it will spend blinks, or the bar shows a hatched chunk if it has to borrow movement. Penalties (Move & Attack, Rapid Strike) appear as badges in the readout before you commit.

Why not pips for movement too?

Ten movement pips after a 3.25 m walk: you get a half-pip nobody can read at a glance, ten pips competing with the two that actually matter, and the dots you already rejected for the Move bar. Pips suit things you spend whole; a bar suits things you spend by the metre.

When there are many pips

If AP slices the one-second turn into equal ticks, every pool is made of the same unit, and the pips join into one strip: the whole second, left to right. Below, a second is 6 AP, which splits cleanly into halves and thirds (a step is 1 AP, half your Move is 2 AP). The number is illustrative; the strip works the same at 4, 8 or 12.

  • One strip, grouped by pool. Movement first (gold), then Action (bone), then Attack (red). A small gap between pools, never between pips of one pool.
  • Movement pips drain smoothly. Walking 1.6 AP empties one pip and drains the next to 40%. Joined together, the gold pips read as the Move bar you already have, cut into seconds.
  • Action pips go all at once. Spent ones turn hollow. The pips an action would spend blink before you commit.
  • Borrowing is visible. An action paid from movement takes whole gold pips from the right end, shown as bone outlines inside the gold group.
  • Extra pools attach after a divider with a small label (Extra Attack, Haste). Haste is a second strip under the first, because it's a second turn, not a longer one.
  • Past about 12 pips, ticks group in threes so you can count without counting. At very high totals the group shows a number instead.

Example character: Move 5. Movement 4 AP (1.25 m each), Action 2 AP; a sword swing costs 2 AP.

What it gets right

  • AP is the one-second turn cut into pieces. Man to Man's first draft did exactly this (1 yard = 1 AP, a swing = 2). It was dropped for bookkeeping, which the computer now does.
  • The mobility-for-action trade is GURPS's core trade. Attack gets one step; Move gets full Move. Letting Movement AP buy actions, never the reverse, says "I stood my ground to do more."
  • Extra Attack as Attack AP is faithful. In GURPS it adds attacks inside the Attack maneuver without adding mobility. A dedicated pool is the same thing.
  • Ready and reloads fit AP better than maneuvers. "Three turns of Ready" becomes a cost whose progress carries across turns.

Three fixes before anything is built

1 · The baseline turn is a free Move and Attack

Full Movement AP plus an action lets everyone run full Move and attack at full skill with full defenses. That's D&D's turn.

GURPS Move and Attack: melee −4 with skill capped at 9, ranged −2 or Bulk, no parry, no retreat.

Fix: make it a state worked out from what you did. Moved more than a step this turn and attacked, before or after? The penalties apply until your next turn. A graded version (step: none, up to half Move: −2, beyond: −4) is friendlier than the all-or-nothing cliff.

2 · Movement-funded attacks beat All-Out Attack

Standing still and pouring movement into attacks gives several attacks at full skill while keeping your defenses.

GURPS caps it at two: All-Out Attack (Double) with no defenses, or Rapid Strike at −6 each.

Fix: every attack after the first that isn't paid from Attack AP takes the Rapid Strike penalty. Attacks paid from Attack AP take none, which gives Extra Attack a clear job.

3 · Speed becomes an attack stat

If the Movement pool equals Move, a Move 8 demon gets more actions than a Move 5 human, and a flier on Air Move (2× ground) gets a doubled pool to turn into attacks.

GURPS: Move never grants extra attacks.

Fix: everyone gets the same Movement AP pool; Move sets metres per AP. Fliers pay less AP per metre. Encumbrance and size then change distance only, as in GURPS.

The open questions, answered

  • Defenses: off AP entirely. Dodge is free and unlimited in GURPS; Parry and Block have their own limits, which already reset on your own turn. Putting them on AP means a choice every time you're attacked.
  • Reaction pool: none. Model GURPS Wait instead: hold leftover Action or Attack AP at end of turn for a triggered action, like a Stop Thrust. GURPS has no free opportunity attacks.
  • Unused Movement AP: no carry-over, but it funds Retreat: one step back when you defend, for +3 Dodge or +1 Parry/Block, once per turn. Leftover movement gets a defensive use without inventing anything.
  • Refill: every pool refills at the start of your own turn.
  • All-Out Attack: a toggle declared before your first attack; Determined (+4 hit), Strong (+2 damage) or Double (one extra attack with no Rapid Strike penalty). Half Move cap, no defenses until your next turn.
  • All-Out Defense: only if you haven't attacked; locks out attacks for the turn. Half Move cap; +2 to one defense or Double Defense.
  • Varying costs: by action type, and by a weapon's ready-after-use cost, not a swing-speed stat (GURPS has none). Attributes act only through Basic Speed setting metres per AP.

Missing pieces

  • A free step (about 1 m) that never triggers Move and Attack.
  • Posture changes and standing up as AP costs; this matters once shove-to-prone lands.
  • Feint: a Quick Contest of skills that spends an attack.
  • Bad footing costing extra Movement AP per metre (×2 in GURPS), so Grease and Web plug in.
  • Sprinting: extra distance for FP.
  • AI planning: free ordering grows the plan space. Choose position and main action, then fill leftover AP greedily.

Simplifications

  • No baseline Attack AP. Everyone gets Movement AP plus a small Action pool; Attack AP exists only through Extra Attack. Without it, a baseline Attack pool is just Action AP with a different colour.
  • Fractional AP internally, whole numbers on screen. Pathing stays on 0.25 m cells; only the bar rounds.
  • Derive commitments where possible. Move and Attack is worked out from what you did; only All-Out Attack and All-Out Defense are declared.
  • One spending rule: the matching check. Priority order becomes a tie-break.

The current economy is already halfway there: movement is a metre budget, Ability.move_cost already lets actions eat movement, attack options are stacking toggles, and defenses already live outside the turn. The conversion is pools in place of the single attack action, the Move and Attack state, the Rapid Strike penalty, per-turn Aim, and teaching ai_scorer to price AP.

Numbers in the mockup are illustrative: Movement pool 10 AP (Move 5, so 2 AP per metre), an action borrowed from movement costs 4 AP, one Action AP, one Attack AP.
