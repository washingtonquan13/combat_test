# Cutscenes: the minimal path

This replaces the earlier simplification plan. That plan tidied the automated system; this one stops
building on it.

## Why

The cutscene system was built automation-first: cameras that orbit and follow roles, a framing checker,
paired actions that line two bodies up wherever they happen to stand, layered body animation, and scenes
generated by scripts. Hand authoring arrived last and was bolted on. It was also built ahead of content:
six milestones landed before anyone made a scene by hand, so the friction never showed up.

Cutscenes are normally made by hand: place the people, place the cameras by eye, key a timeline, watch,
adjust. That is what this plan builds, using Godot's own tools wherever they already do the job.

**The test for the whole plan:** making a cutscene should feel like using Godot, not like learning a
framework.

## What a cutscene is

One `.tscn`. Inside it:

| Thing | What it is | Godot tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Timeline** | One AnimationPlayer. Everything below is keyed on it. | Animation panel |
| **Roles** | A body per character, standing where you put it. You move it, turn it and play its clips by keying it. | Node3D + the body's own AnimationPlayer |
| **Cameras** | Plain Camera3D nodes. Fly the viewport, then *Align Transform with View*. Key their transform for a move, their FOV for a zoom. | Camera3D, Preview checkbox |
| **Cuts** | One track that says which camera is live. | A discrete property track |
| **Clips** | The body's animation, keyed on an Animation Playback track. Scrubbing previews it. | Animation Playback track |
| **Walks** | Key the body's position at the start and end, with a walk clip playing between. A curved walk follows a Path3D. | Position track, or Path3D + PathFollow3D with `progress` keyed |
| **Lines, sounds, effects** | Keys on a Call Method track at the moment they happen. Sounds can also be Audio tracks. | Call Method / Audio tracks |

Everything you see while scrubbing is the timeline itself, so the editor and the game play the same
keys. There is no second preview implementation to drift out of step.

## The small game-specific layer

Only five things are not stock Godot:

1. **Playing a scene.** `CinematicDirector.play(scene)` stays exactly as it is: an awaited call, skip
   is not abort, and the scene reports whether it finished. The callers (area entry scenes, fusion,
   dialogue staging, the ambush) do not change.
2. **Who plays a role.** In the editor a role shows a body you pick. In game the role's body is dressed
   as the real unit (its model, clothes, hair, weapon), the real unit is hidden for the scene, and at
   the end the real unit is put where the body finished and shown again. The timeline only ever
   animates the scene's own bodies, which is what keeps editor and game the same. (This is the stand-in
   idea we already have, made the only way roles work.)
3. **Lines that wait.** A line key shows the text, and if it waits for the player the timeline pauses
   until they advance.
4. **Skip.** Jumps the timeline to its last frame, so everyone ends where the scene puts them.
5. **Spawning.** A role can be someone the scene brings into the world (the stranger arriving) and
   leaves behind when it ends.

That is the whole runtime. Each piece gets a short paragraph in the tutorial, not a chapter.

## Paired actions (a kill, a grab, a blow)

**Handled the way films and games normally handle them: authored together, played together.**

- In Blender, both characters' clips are made or adjusted in one file, against each other, so the blade
  lands in the chest. That is where the alignment work belongs.
- They are exported as two clips that share one origin.
- In the scene, both roles stand on the same mark, and the two clips are keyed at the same time.
- The hit sound and the blood are keys at the contact frame.

The current PairedActionBeat exists to line up two bodies that arrive from gameplay at arbitrary
positions. A cutscene controls where everyone stands, so that problem does not exist here. A scene that
starts from wherever a fight ended opens on a cut, and the cut hides everyone being put on their
marks. That is standard practice.

## What becomes optional or retires

Nothing here is deleted until no scene uses it.

| Piece | Fate |
|---|---|
| Orbit / follow camera rigs | Retire from cutscenes. They may still serve dialogue cameras. |
| Framing checker | Retire. Framing is judged by eye. |
| CameraPathRig | Retire. Path3D + PathFollow3D does this natively. |
| PairedClip, PairedActionBeat, ContactEvent, ImpactKit | Retire. See above. |
| Beat nodes (Walk, Face, Place, Clip, Line, Spawn...) | Retire in favour of keys. Line and Spawn become method keys. |
| Layered body animation (BodyAnimationTree) in cutscenes | Retire unless a scene shows it needs a layer. |
| Scenes generated by script (the camp raid) | Remade by hand, or kept on the old system until remade. |
| Dialogue's procedural cameras | **Kept.** Many conversations with automatic coverage is where automation earns its place. Out of scope here. |

## Order of work

Each step is small and ends in something you can try.

1. **Make one real scene the minimal way, by hand, with no new code beyond the five-item layer above.**
   You pick the scene. Everything that gets in the way while making it goes on a list.
2. **Fix the list.** Only what that scene hit. Likely candidates: a button that makes a new camera from
   the viewport, and a "Play from playhead" that runs the scene in game from the cursor.
3. **Remake the duel the minimal way** and compare it with the current one side by side. This proves
   the minimal path covers a fight, including the kill.
4. **Decide the other scenes one by one**: remake, or delete. Several are tests (`fight_test`,
   `paired_stab`, `test_arena_arrival`, `demo_conversation`).
5. **Retire the old pieces** once nothing uses them, along with their tests. The tutorial shrinks to
   match.

The old system keeps playing its scenes untouched until step 5. Nothing working breaks along the way.

## Open questions for you

- **Which scene first?** It should be one you actually want in the game. Recommended: a short
  two-person scene with a line or two and a few cuts, then the duel in step 3.
- **Dressed bodies in the editor?** Recommended: not at first. Pick a preview body the right size and
  check the dressed look with Play from playhead. Making the dressing code run in the editor is a
  separate job, worth doing only if the undressed preview bothers you in practice.
- **Blending between clips.** Animation Playback tracks switch clips instantly, so a clip change in the
  middle of a shot can pop. Recommended: change clips on camera cuts or on still poses first, and add a
  blend only when a scene needs one.
- **Keep or delete the test scenes?** Recommended: delete the tests, remake the content scenes.

## Appendix for the programmer

- **Keep:** `cinematic_director.gd` and its `play()` contract; the stand-in body code from `stand_in.gd`;
  `cinematic_overlay` for lines; the skip handling.
- **New:** a lean stage root script. It resolves roles to stand-ins, picks the live camera from the cut
  track, and offers `say()` and `spawn()` as method-key targets. Its size target is a few hundred lines,
  not 1,352.
- **Retire at step 5:** `camera_framing.gd`, `framing_check.gd`, `framing_rig.gd`, `camera_path_rig.gd`,
  `paired_clip.gd`, `contact_event.gd`, `impact_kit.gd`, `beats/`, `scene_template.gd`, `scene_cast.gd`
  and `scene_binding.gd` (only if dialogue staging can move to the new role binding), the old
  `cinematic_stage.gd`, and `tests/cinematic/*` (replaced by one test that plays a minimal scene to its
  end and one that skips it).
- **Dialogue staging** (`dialogue_staging.gd`) calls `CinematicDirector.play()` with a cast. Check
  that path against the new role binding before retiring `SceneCast`.
